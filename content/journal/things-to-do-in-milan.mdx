Milan scored 87 out of 100 on my Europe 2026 trip, the highest of any city I've rated. I got everywhere on the metro, watched one of the best games of football I've ever seen, and found out I love cannoli. I'll definitely go back.

## What to do

<Picks>
  <Pick name="Go up on the Duomo" tag="Must do" price="About €20 by stairs" href="https://www.duomomilano.it/en/">
    Milan's Gothic cathedral took almost six centuries to finish. On the rooftop terraces you walk among the spires and statues with the city below you. It's the largest church in Italy (St. Peter's is bigger, but that's in Vatican City). I booked ahead and took the stairs, which is cheaper than the lift.
  </Pick>
  <Pick name="See a game at San Siro" tag="Football" price="My seat: €129.90" href="https://www.inter.it/en/tickets">
    I watched Inter come from behind to beat Fiorentina 2–1 in front of 74,642 people, the best atmosphere of any game I've been to. I bought my ticket on Inter's official site after creating an account. AC Milan play here too: [AC Milan tickets](https://www.acmilan.com/en/tickets).
  </Pick>
  <Pick name="Walk through Castello Sforzesco" tag="History" price="Courtyards free · museums €10" href="https://www.milanocastello.it/en">
    The 15th-century castle of the Sforza family, who once ruled Milan. It holds Michelangelo's last sculpture, the Rondanini Pietà, which he was still working on days before he died.
  </Pick>
  <Pick name="Take a day trip to St. Moritz" tag="Day trip" price="$134.83" href="https://www.getyourguide.com/en-au/milan-l139/from-milan-bernina-train-swiss-alps-st-moritz-day-trip-t866729/">
    A coach from Milano Centrale at 7:30, past Lake Como and over the Maloja Pass, about an hour and a half of free time in St. Moritz, then the red Bernina Express train down to Tirano and a coach back. A long day, with most of it spent on the coach and train.
  </Pick>
</Picks>

## Where and what to eat

<Picks>
  <Pick name="The No Diet Club food tour" tag="Best moment" price="From about €55" href="https://www.nodiet.club/milan-food-tour">
    The best moment of my trip until Chelsea beat Manchester United in London. A three-hour walking tour from Corso di Porta Romana, with all the food included. I booked it on [GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/en-au/milan-l139/no-diet-club-unique-local-food-tour-in-milan--t527893/). Two of its stops are worth going back to on your own:
  </Pick>
  <Pick name="Cannoli at Ammu" tag="On the food tour" price="Corso di Porta Romana">
    A Sicilian pastry: a crisp fried shell, filled with sweetened ricotta while you wait. I didn't expect anything made with cheese to taste like that. They come close to a Portuguese pastel de nata, and I'll be making them at home.
  </Pick>
  <Pick name="Pizza by the slice at Rom'antica" tag="On the food tour" price="Corso di Porta Ticinese">
    Roman-style pizza al taglio, sold by weight. I had the tuna and onion.
  </Pick>
  <Pick name="Breakfast at Boscovich" tag="Breakfast">
    French toast with berries and pistachios, and a hot chocolate, before I left for Rome.
  </Pick>
  <Pick name="Osteria Al 29" tag="Dinner">
    Proper Italian food (pasta, gnocchi and pizza) in big portions at good prices.
  </Pick>
  <Pick name="Aperitivo on the Navigli" tag="Evening">
    Naviglio Grande is lined with restaurants, wine bars and terraces, one of the best places in Milan for dinner at sunset; Naviglio Pavese, next to it, is quieter. For cocktails: Mag Cafè, Rita & Cocktails or Ugo. Officina 12 does modern Italian food, and El Brellin serves classic Milanese dishes in an old courtyard.
  </Pick>
</Picks>

**Gifts to take home:** panettone, Milan's sweet bread, sold beautifully boxed all year, and saffron for risotto alla Milanese, which is light and easy to pack.

## Getting there and around

I flew into **Linate**, the airport closest to the city, took the M4 metro to Milano Dateo, and changed there to the S5 suburban train into the city. The S5 didn't take tap-to-pay, so I had to buy a ticket for it. After that I went everywhere on the metro, just tapping my phone or contactless bank card at the gates. The [ATM website](https://www.atm.it/en/) has fares and maps.

## Where to stay

I stayed at **Hotel Berna**, a few minutes' walk from Milano Centrale. Good location, but an older room, and being near the main station makes trains (and the St. Moritz coach) easy.

## Good to know

- **Be careful on trains.**
- **Pistachio gelato should be brownish or a pale, pastel green.** If a shop's pistachio is bright green, it's been coloured, and the shop isn't the real thing.
- **Buy a ticket for the S trains.** The S5 from Dateo didn't take tap-to-pay. On the metro, tapping your phone or contactless card works.
- **Book the Duomo ahead**, and take the stairs to the rooftop to save a few euros.

## Weather

Late October was mild: around 13–18°C (55–64°F) in the day and 6–10°C (43–50°F) at night, and dry apart from light rain on the day I arrived.

## Would I go back?

Yes. It's the best city I've been to so far, for the football, the food and how easy it is to get around. Next time I'd stay a night longer and spend a day at Lake Como.
