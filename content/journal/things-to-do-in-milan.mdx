Milan scored 87 out of 100 on my Europe 2026 trip, the highest of any city I've rated. I got everywhere on the metro, watched one of the best games of football I've ever seen, and found out I love cannoli. I'll definitely go back.

## How long to stay

I spent four nights there, including a day trip to St. Moritz in Switzerland. I'd recommend five, so you also have a day for Lake Como.

My four nights looked like this:

- **Day 1:** arrived.
- **Day 2:** the Duomo and its rooftop, cannoli at Ammu and pizza at Rom'antica.
- **Day 3:** Castello Sforzesco, and Inter v Fiorentina at San Siro.
- **Day 4:** a day trip to St. Moritz.
- **Day 5:** breakfast at Boscovich, then the train to Rome.

## Getting there and around

I flew into **Linate**, the airport closest to the city, and took the metro from the airport into the centre. Contactless payment is meant to work at the gates, but my phone (Apple Pay) didn't, so have a contactless bank card or a ticket from the ATM app ready. After that I went everywhere on the metro.

## Where to stay

I stayed at **Hotel Berna**, a few minutes' walk from Milano Centrale. It's a good hotel, and being near the main station makes trains easy.

## Book ahead

- **The Duomo:** I booked my ticket ahead on the [official Duomo website](https://www.duomomilano.it/en/) and took the stairs to the rooftop, which is cheaper than the lift.
- **Inter:** I bought my ticket on [Inter's official website](https://www.inter.it/en/tickets) after creating an account there.
- **St. Moritz:** I booked the [Bernina Train, Swiss Alps and St. Moritz day trip from Milan](https://www.getyourguide.com/en-au/milan-l139/from-milan-bernina-train-swiss-alps-st-moritz-day-trip-t866729/) on GetYourGuide: a coach to St. Moritz, the red Bernina Express train through the Alps, and a coach back to Milan.

## Budget and weather

I spent about €50 a day on food, eating two meals a day.

Late October was mild: around 13–18°C in the day and 6–10°C at night, and dry apart from light rain on the day I arrived.

## What to do

**Go up on the Duomo.** Milan's Gothic cathedral took almost six centuries to finish, and the rooftop terraces are the highlight: you walk among the spires and statues with the whole city below you. Go inside too. It's the largest church in Italy (St. Peter's is bigger, but that's in Vatican City).

**See a game at San Siro.** I watched Inter come from behind to beat Fiorentina 2–1 in front of 74,642 people: Thuram equalised in the 91st minute and Zieliński scored a 98th-minute penalty to win it. It was the best atmosphere of any game I've been to. Inter and AC Milan share the stadium, so check both clubs' fixtures.

**Walk through Castello Sforzesco.** The 15th-century castle of the Sforza family, who once ruled Milan. It holds Michelangelo's last sculpture, the Rondanini Pietà, which he was still working on days before he died.

**Take a day trip to St. Moritz.** The red Bernina Express train runs through the Swiss Alps (how I booked it is above).

## Where and what to eat

**Do the No Diet Club food tour.** It was the best moment of my trip until Chelsea beat Manchester United in London. It's a three-hour walking tour that starts at midday on Corso di Porta Romana, and it costs from about €55 a person, depending on where you book (check the current price).

**Get cannoli at Ammu** on Corso di Porta Romana. They're a Sicilian pastry: a crisp fried shell, filled with sweetened ricotta while you wait. I didn't expect anything made with cheese to taste like that. They come close to a Portuguese pastel de nata, and I'll be making them at home.

**Have breakfast at Boscovich.** French toast with berries and pistachios, and a hot chocolate, before I left for Rome.

**Get pizza by the slice at Rom'antica** on Corso di Porta Ticinese. It's Roman-style pizza al taglio, sold by weight. I had the tuna and onion.

**Go to Osteria Al 29** for proper Italian food (pasta, gnocchi and pizza) in big portions at good prices.

**Have aperitivo on the Navigli canals.** Naviglio Grande is lined with restaurants, wine bars and terraces, and is one of the best places in Milan for dinner at sunset; Naviglio Pavese, next to it, is quieter. For cocktails, try Mag Cafè, Rita & Cocktails or Ugo. Officina 12 does modern Italian food, and El Brellin serves classic Milanese dishes in an old courtyard.

## More to see

**Museums.** Fondazione Prada for contemporary art, the Museo del Novecento beside the Duomo for 20th-century Italian art, and the Pinacoteca di Brera and Pinacoteca Ambrosiana for Renaissance and Baroque masters.

**Fashion and design.** Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II, the Quadrilatero della Moda around Via Monte Napoleone, Armani/Silos, and Bosco Verticale, the tower blocks covered in trees.

**Lake Como or Lake Lecco.** Trains from Milano Centrale take you there for a day by the water, with beaches to swim at.

**Gifts to take home.** Panettone, and saffron for risotto alla Milanese.

## Would I go back?

Yes. It's the best city I've been to so far, for the football, the food and how easy it is to get around. Next time I'd stay a night longer and spend a day at Lake Como.
