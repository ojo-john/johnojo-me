Milan scored 87 out of 100 on my Europe 2026 trip, the highest of any city I've rated. I got everywhere on the metro, watched one of the best games of football I've ever seen, and found out I love cannoli. I'll definitely go back.

## How long to stay

I spent four nights there, including a day trip to St. Moritz in Switzerland. I'd recommend five, so you also have a day for Lake Como.

My four nights looked like this:

- **Day 1:** arrived.
- **Day 2:** the Duomo and its rooftop in the morning, then the No Diet Club food tour.
- **Day 3:** Inter v Fiorentina at San Siro at lunchtime, then Castello Sforzesco.
- **Day 4:** a day trip to St. Moritz.
- **Day 5:** breakfast at Boscovich, then the train to Rome.

## Getting there and around

I flew into **Linate**, the airport closest to the city, and took the metro from the airport into the centre. Contactless payment is meant to work at the gates, but my phone (Apple Pay) didn't, so have a contactless bank card or a ticket from the ATM app ready. After that I went everywhere on the metro.

## Where to stay

I stayed at **Hotel Berna**, a few minutes' walk from Milano Centrale. It's a good hotel, and being near the main station makes trains easy.

## Book ahead

- **The Duomo:** I booked my ticket ahead on the [official Duomo website](https://www.duomomilano.it/en/) and took the stairs to the rooftop, which is cheaper than the lift.
- **Inter:** I bought my ticket on [Inter's official website](https://www.inter.it/en/tickets) after creating an account there.
- **St. Moritz:** I booked the [Bernina Train, Swiss Alps and St. Moritz day trip from Milan](https://www.getyourguide.com/en-au/milan-l139/from-milan-bernina-train-swiss-alps-st-moritz-day-trip-t866729/) on GetYourGuide. It leaves from Piazza Duca d'Aosta, in front of Milano Centrale (handy from Hotel Berna). A coach takes you past Lake Como and over the Maloja Pass to St. Moritz, where you get about an hour and a half of free time. Then it's about two and a half hours on the red Bernina Express train over the Bernina Pass and through Poschiavo down to Tirano, and a coach back to Milan. It's a long day, with most of it spent on the coach and train.

## Budget

What four nights cost me, with prices for the things I booked:

- **Hotel:** Hotel Berna, $488 for four nights including taxes (about $122 a night). Good location, but an older room.
- **Food:** about €50 a day, eating two meals a day.
- **Getting around:** €2.20 a ride on the metro, valid for 90 minutes, including from Linate airport.
- **The Duomo:** about €20 for the rooftop by stairs.
- **No Diet Club food tour:** from about €55.
- **Castello Sforzesco museums:** €10.
- **Inter v Fiorentina:** €129.90, for a seat in the central second tier (secondo rosso centrale).
- **St. Moritz day trip:** $134.83. The coach leaves at 7:30 in the morning.

## Weather

Late October was mild: around 13–18°C in the day and 6–10°C at night, and dry apart from light rain on the day I arrived.

## What to do

**Go up on the [Duomo](https://www.duomomilano.it/en/).** Milan's Gothic cathedral took almost six centuries to finish, and the rooftop terraces are the highlight: you walk among the spires and statues with the whole city below you. Go inside too. It's the largest church in Italy (St. Peter's is bigger, but that's in Vatican City). Book ahead; the rooftop by stairs costs about €20 and is cheaper than the lift.

**See a game at San Siro.** I watched Inter come from behind to beat Fiorentina 2–1 in front of 74,642 people: Thuram equalised in the 91st minute and Zieliński scored a 98th-minute penalty to win it. It was the best atmosphere of any game I've been to. Inter and AC Milan share the stadium, so check both clubs' fixtures and buy from the official sites: [Inter](https://www.inter.it/en/tickets) or [AC Milan](https://www.acmilan.com/en/tickets). You'll need to create an account.

**Walk through [Castello Sforzesco](https://www.milanocastello.it/en).** The 15th-century castle of the Sforza family, who once ruled Milan. It holds Michelangelo's last sculpture, the Rondanini Pietà, which he was still working on days before he died. The courtyards are free; the museums inside are €10.

**Take a [day trip to St. Moritz](https://www.getyourguide.com/en-au/milan-l139/from-milan-bernina-train-swiss-alps-st-moritz-day-trip-t866729/).** You ride the red Bernina Express train through the Swiss Alps. The route is above.

## Where and what to eat

**Do the No Diet Club food tour.** It was the best moment of my trip until Chelsea beat Manchester United in London. It's a three-hour walking tour from Corso di Porta Romana, and it costs from about €55 a person, depending on where you book (check the current price). Two of its stops:

**Cannoli at Ammu** on Corso di Porta Romana. They're a Sicilian pastry: a crisp fried shell, filled with sweetened ricotta while you wait. I didn't expect anything made with cheese to taste like that. They come close to a Portuguese pastel de nata, and I'll be making them at home.

**Pizza by the slice at Rom'antica** on Corso di Porta Ticinese. It's Roman-style pizza al taglio, sold by weight. I had the tuna and onion.

You can also go to both on your own if you don't do the tour.

**Have breakfast at Boscovich.** French toast with berries and pistachios, and a hot chocolate, before I left for Rome.

**Go to Osteria Al 29** for proper Italian food (pasta, gnocchi and pizza) in big portions at good prices.

**Have aperitivo on the Navigli canals.** Naviglio Grande is lined with restaurants, wine bars and terraces, and is one of the best places in Milan for dinner at sunset; Naviglio Pavese, next to it, is quieter. For cocktails, try Mag Cafè, Rita & Cocktails or Ugo. Officina 12 does modern Italian food, and El Brellin serves classic Milanese dishes in an old courtyard.

## More to see

Prices are adult tickets as of late 2026; check before you go.

### Museums

- **[Fondazione Prada](https://www.fondazioneprada.org/servizio-visitatori-en/?lang=en)**: contemporary art in a converted industrial complex. €15.
- **[Museo del Novecento](https://www.museodelnovecento.org/)**: 20th-century Italian art, right beside the Duomo. €10, free on the first Sunday of the month.
- **[Pinacoteca di Brera](https://pinacotecabrera.org/en/)**: Renaissance and Baroque masters, including Raphael and Caravaggio. €15.
- **[Pinacoteca Ambrosiana](https://www.ambrosiana.it/en/)**: more Renaissance art, with a Caravaggio of its own. About €17.

### Fashion and design

- **Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II**: the glass-roofed shopping arcade next to the Duomo. Free to walk through.
- **Quadrilatero della Moda**: the luxury fashion streets around Via Monte Napoleone. Free to wander.
- **[Armani/Silos](https://www.armanisilos.com/)**: decades of Giorgio Armani's collections, by timed entry. €12.
- **Bosco Verticale**: the two tower blocks covered in trees. Free to see from the park below.

### A day at the lakes

- **[Lake Como](https://www.trenord.it/en/)**: trains from Milano Centrale to Como San Giovanni take about 40 minutes and cost about €5 each way.
- **Varenna and Lierna**: on the Lecco arm of Lake Como. Trains from Milano Centrale take about an hour to Varenna and cost about €7. There are beaches to swim at around Lierna.

### Gifts to take home

- **Panettone**, Milan's sweet bread, sold beautifully boxed all year.
- **Saffron**, for risotto alla Milanese. It's light and easy to pack.

## Would I go back?

Yes. It's the best city I've been to so far, for the football, the food and how easy it is to get around. Next time I'd stay a night longer and spend a day at Lake Como.
