Every trip begins with a decision made on partial information. You book the flight before you know what the weather will do, which streets will matter, or who you will meet. The writer of Hebrews takes that ordinary experience and makes it a picture of faith.

<Scripture k="Hebrews 11:8-10" />

## Called before he knew

Abraham’s obedience comes before his understanding. The call in <Ref k="Genesis 12:1">Genesis 12:1</Ref> names what he must leave, his country, his relatives and his father’s house, but only promises a land “that I will show you.” The destination is held back on purpose.<Fn n={1} />

We usually want the itinerary first. Hebrews describes a faith that moves on the strength of the one who calls, not on the completeness of the plan.

## At home as a foreigner

Verse 9 says he lived “as an alien in the land of promise.” The word describes someone who lives alongside a place without being rooted in it.<Fn n={2} /> He was in the right place and still lived in tents.

<Pull>He was in the right place and still lived in tents.</Pull>

Later in the chapter the same people are called “strangers and pilgrims on the earth” in <Ref k="Hebrews 11:13">Hebrews 11:13</Ref>. The tent is not a failure of faith. It is what faith looks like before the promise is complete.

## Looking for a city

The tent and the city are set against each other. A tent can be packed in an hour, but a city has foundations. Abraham could live lightly because he was looking for something permanent, a city “whose builder and maker is God.”<Fn n={3} />

The chapter returns to this in <Ref k="Hebrews 11:16">Hebrews 11:16</Ref>: “he has prepared a city for them.” The journey has a destination even when the traveller cannot yet see it.

## For the road

<Ref k="Psalm 121:8">Psalm 121:8</Ref> was written for pilgrims climbing toward Jerusalem: “Yahweh will keep your going out and your coming in.” It covers both halves of every trip.
